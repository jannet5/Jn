# Tells in landing copy, English and Spanish

Read this at step 11 of the process, before you hand the copy over, and any
time a line sounds like it could sit on anyone's page. Every phrase that
`landing-skills:landing-review` flags in copy is covered here, with what to
write instead.

## How to use this list

These phrases are tells because they are the median of a vast amount of
marketing text, so a model reaches for them first and a reader has seen them
everywhere. Swapping one for a synonym does not help: the synonym is the next
median. The fix is always the same. Replace the phrase with a fact from the
brief: what the product does, to what, for whom, with which number or name.

The rule for verbs: a verb needs a concrete object the product acts on.
"Streamline your workflow" has no object you can picture. "Turn staff
availability into a draft rota" does.

A word on this list is not banned in every sentence. "Robust" in a load
rating with a number is a claim; "robust platform" is padding. The question
is whether the sentence still says something checkable when you remove the
word.

Sources: the English and Spanish lists come from writing-tell catalogues
(Wikipedia's guide to signs of AI writing, practitioner and detector guides,
Spanish articles listing the words ChatGPT overuses). The Spanish evidence is
mostly about general text, not landing pages. Rows marked "judgement" are
this skill's own call, with the reason given, and have no source.

## The swap test

Replace the company name with a competitor's. If the sentence still works, it
is generic, and it fails. A Spanish copywriting guide puts it as copy that
would fit a hair salon, a yoga school or a biscuit shop equally well. Rewrite
until the sentence could only be about this business: its product, place,
price, process or client.

| Fails the swap test | Passes |
|---|---|
| We make your project a reality. | We change locks in [area] and hand you every key we cut. |
| Calidad y compromiso en cada trabajo. | Grupos de seis en piscina temperada, en Concepción. |

Run it on the headline, the subhead, every section heading and every line of
reassurance.

When a line fails, first look harder at the brief. A concrete action with
its number, a visible result, a place or a price often sits in the brief
while the headline describes only the category. Lead with that.

When the brief really holds nothing only this business has, keep the plain,
true line. Do not invent a differentiator to pass the test: an invented one
is a false claim, and a plain line is only a weak one. Then list in the open
questions of `landing/copy.md` exactly what would make the line specific:
the method, the scope, the place, who does the work, a number.

## English: inflated verbs

| Phrase | Why it reads as generated | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| unlock the power / unlock the full potential, unlock | Verb with no object; promotional puffery | What the reader can now do: "See which shifts are still uncovered." |
| elevate your ... | Same | The change, measured: "Plan next week's rota in 10 minutes" (only with a figure the brief gives). |
| empower your team / empowers you | Same | Who does what: "Staff swap shifts from their phone." |
| supercharge | Same | The specific speed-up, with the brief's number, or the step that disappears. |
| streamline, leverage, harness | Same | Name the steps that go away. |
| revolutionize / revolutionise | A claim nobody can check | What is different from the old way, concretely. |
| take your X to the next level | Stock phrase with no level named | The level: the outcome the brief names. |
| delve into | Listed among the commonest generated-text words | "Read", "see", "look at", or cut the sentence. |

## English: empty adjectives and nouns

| Phrase | Why it reads as generated | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| seamless, seamlessly | Replaceable by any competitor's name | Name the joint that does not show: "An approved swap goes straight into the rota." |
| cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-gen | Same | The actual technique or spec from the brief, or nothing. |
| game-changer, game-changing | Same | What changed, for whom. |
| robust, powerful, effortless | Same | The number or the demonstration: capacity, steps, time. |
| synergy, holistic, end-to-end | Same | List what is covered: "cleaning, gutters and the yearly boiler check". |
| rich tapestry, landscape, realm, journey, beacon | Spatial abstractions that generated text favours | Plain nouns: the market, the field, the process, the steps. |

## English: openers, hooks and metaphors

| Phrase | Why it reads as generated | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| In today's fast-paced (digital) world | Global opener cliché | Start with the reader's situation: "You build next week's rota by hand every Sunday." |
| Let's dive in | Same | Cut it; start with the first fact. |
| Here's the kicker | Staged reveal | State the point. |
| Picture this | Same | Describe the real case directly. |

## Spanish: verbos inflados

The research catalogue describes the Spanish verbs, adjectives and openers
below as calques of English marketing words and lists them as overused by
ChatGPT in general text, not in landing pages specifically. Where a row
names a specific English origin that the research does not give, it is
marked (juicio).

| Frase | Por qué suena generada | Escribe en su lugar |
|---|---|---|
| Desbloquea el poder / todo el potencial, desbloquea | Calco de "unlock"; verbo sin objeto | Lo que el lector puede hacer ahora: "Ves qué turnos quedan sin cubrir." |
| Potencia tu negocio / tu marca | Calco del inglés (el origen exacto, "empower" o "boost", es juicio) | Qué cambia y cuánto, con el dato del brief. |
| Revoluciona tu forma de... | Afirmación que nadie puede comprobar (juicio) | Qué es distinto del método anterior, en concreto. |
| Lleva tu negocio al siguiente nivel | Frase hecha; la investigación no la pudo verificar como ejemplo de landing (juicio) | El resultado concreto que el brief nombra. |
| Eleva, Domina, Desata | Calcos del inglés, según el catálogo (el origen de cada uno es juicio) | Un verbo con objeto: "calcula", "timbra", "reparamos". |
| Sumérgete en, Embárcate en | Calcos del inglés, según el catálogo | Corta la frase y empieza por el dato. |
| Ahonda en, Navega | Están en la lista de la investigación sin explicación (juicio: mismo origen) | "Revisa", "mira", "elige". |

## Spanish: adjetivos vacíos y aperturas

| Frase | Por qué suena generada | Escribe en su lugar |
|---|---|---|
| de vanguardia, innovador, transformador | Lista calcada del inglés; sirve a cualquier empresa | La técnica, el material o la cifra del brief. |
| sin costuras, de forma fluida | "sin costuras" está en el catálogo; "de forma fluida" es juicio, como calco de "seamlessly" | Di qué paso desaparece: "La nómina sale con los recibos ya timbrados." |
| en constante evolución, vibrante, robusto | Adjetivos sin contenido verificable | Qué cambió y cuándo, o nada. |
| sinergia, tapiz, paisaje, reino, cambio de juego | Están en la lista de la investigación; que sean calcos de tapestry, landscape, realm y game changer es juicio | Sustantivos simples: el mercado, el proceso, el cambio. |
| En la era digital, En el mundo actual, En un mundo donde... | Traducción de las aperturas globales del inglés | Empieza por la situación del lector: "Tienes 40 años y nunca aprendiste a nadar." |
| Cuando se trata de... | Muletilla de apertura | Di el tema directamente. |
| Es importante señalar que, Vale la pena señalar que, Es importante tener en cuenta | Andamiaje de cautela | Corta la muletilla y deja el hecho. |
| En resumen, En conclusión, Un testimonio de | Cierres de redacción escolar | Corta; la última frase de la sección ya cierra. |

## Spanish: muletillas de servicio

The first row is in the review detector because unguided agents produce it
for service businesses; the rest are this skill's judgement.

| Frase | Por qué falla | Escribe en su lugar |
|---|---|---|
| Tú decides, nosotros... (con cualquier final) | Reparto de papeles genérico: falla la prueba de cambiar el nombre | Qué hace la empresa y qué hace el cliente, con los pasos del brief. |
| Soluciones integrales, a tu medida (juicio) | Promete todo sin nombrar nada | La lista concreta de lo que incluye el servicio. |
| Te acompañamos en cada paso (juicio) | Igual | Los pasos, con quién y cuándo. |
| Somos un equipo de profesionales apasionados (juicio) | Habla del que vende, sin dato | Quién es, cuántos años lleva, qué ha hecho, si el brief lo da. |
| Calidad y confianza, calidad y compromiso (juicio) | Dupla de valores sin prueba | La prueba: años, trabajos hechos, garantía, del brief. |
| ¡Este es tu momento! | Entusiasmo de autoayuda | Una invitación tranquila y concreta: "Elige tu caja." |

## Structures that read as generated, in both languages

| Structure | Example | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| Negation pivot | "It's not a tool, it's a partner." / "No se trata de X, se trata de Y" / "No es X, es Y" / "No X. No Y. Just Z." | Say Y only, as a plain statement. |
| Triads | "Fast. Simple. Secure." / "rápido, confiable y escalable" | One specific claim. If three things are true and different, give each its own sentence with its evidence. |
| Fragment pairs | "Less noise. More focus." / "Build faster. Ship smarter." | A sentence with the facts: "A 10-class pass costs 120 EUR." |
| Hook questions | "The catch?" / "¿La trampa?", "¿El detalle clave?", "¿La verdad brutal?" | A plain statement. Real questions belong in the FAQ, in the reader's words. |
| Em-dash habit | "Build faster — without sacrificing quality" | Period, comma or colon. Zero em dashes in headlines and buttons. |
| Subhead repeats the headline | Headline "Simple bookkeeping", subhead "Bookkeeping made simple for you" | The subhead says how, for whom, or the proof. |
| Section intro restates the title | "Nuestras cajas" followed by "¿Quieres comer más sano pero no sabes cómo? Aquí te lo contamos." | Start the section with its first fact. |
| Generic headings | Features, Everything you need, Why choose us, Nuestros servicios, Our story | A heading named after the job (see `structure.md`). |
| Title Case headings | "How It Works For You" | Sentence case. In Spanish, title case is also wrong by the language's own rules. |
| Vague attribution | "studies show", "industry reports", "experts agree", "experts argue", "observers have cited" | Name the source, or give your own measured figure, or cut the claim. |
| Curly quotes everywhere | A weak signal some detectors cite | Keep whichever the brand's typography uses, consistently. |

## Proof tells

| Tell | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Trusted by 10,000+ teams" with no basis, "Loved by teams", "as seen in" | Unverifiable; generated pages produce it by default | Real figures from the brief, or nothing. |
| Logo wall as the only proof | Same | Real, permitted logos with a number or milestone beside them. |
| Round-number stat row: 99.9%, 10x, 24/7, 10k+, 50+, 4.9/5 | A stats band filled because pages "have one" | Only sourced figures, with their context and baseline. |
| Stock testimonial names (Sarah Johnson, John Smith, John Doe, Jane Smith), first-name-only quotes, generated avatars | Plausible-generic; fake testimonials are also banned in the US | Real named customers with permission, or a `[PLACEHOLDER: ...]`, or no section. |
| Unsourced percentages and vague counts ("95% uplift", "hundreds of clients") | No baseline, no number | "[number] clients", "cut from [x] to [y]", from the brief only. |

Fabricated proof is not only a style problem: see `proof-and-pricing.md` for
the legal position.

## Call-to-action tells

| Tell | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Submit", "Learn more", "Get started" as the only label | Verb plus outcome: "Book a 20-minute demo", "Reservar cupo". |
| An arrow glyph at the end of the label | Drop it; the label says what happens. |
| The same label six times, or several labels for one action | One label for the primary action at its main places (a shorter form of it in the header is fine); one different, lower-commitment secondary action. |
| A button with nothing under it about what happens next | One line saying first what the click does; cost, requirements or reply time only as an addition, and only from the brief. |
| Emoji as bullets or icons (rocket, sparkles, light bulb, lightning) | Words, or one coherent icon set chosen by the art direction. |

## Spanish-specific tells

The full rules for writing Spanish are in `headlines.md`, "Writing in
Spanish". The tells that show when they are broken:

- A line-by-line translation of English copy: English rhythm, English stock
  phrases, English length.
- tú and usted mixed on one page, often usted in the form and tú in the hero.
- Two narrators: "nosotros" in one section, the business in the third person
  in the next.
- Regional vocabulary mixed on one page.
- English leftovers: "Features", "Pricing", "Get started", "Submit" in
  buttons, form hints or headings.
- Title Case headings and missing ¿ or ¡.
- Dollar prices written with $ alone for a reader in a peso country, who may
  read pesos (judgement; see the number formats in `headlines.md`).

## Placeholder leftovers

Demo text that reaches a live page tells every reader nobody proofread it:
lorem ipsum, "Your Company Name", bracketed prompts starting with "Your" or
"Insert", a leftover line about being an AI language model, a default page
title such as "My App", "Vite + React" or "Create Next App". The review
detector flags each of these.

Your own placeholders use only the form `[PLACEHOLDER: what is needed]`,
which the detector does not flag and which the review lists as open items
instead.

## Coverage of the review detector

`landing-skills:landing-review` runs a static scan whose copy rules match the
phrases below. Each is covered above. The lists in this file are longer than
the detector's, so a clean scan does not mean the copy is free of tells.

| Rule | Matches | Covered in |
|---|---|---|
| `copy-inflated-en` | unlock the (full) power/potential; seamless, seamlessly; elevate your; supercharge; take your ... to the next level; in today's fast-paced; game-changer, game-changing; revolutionize, revolutionise; cutting-edge; let's dive in; here's the kicker; picture this; delve into; rich tapestry; empower/empowers/empowering your, you or teams | English sections |
| `copy-inflated-es` | desbloquea el/todo el poder/potencial; lleva tu ... al siguiente nivel; revoluciona tu; de forma fluida; en el mundo actual; en un mundo donde; en constante evolución; sumérgete en; embárcate en; tú decides, nosotros | Spanish sections |
| `copy-negation-pivot` | it's not (just/only) X, it's; No X. No Y. Just; no se trata de X, se trata de | Structures |
| `vague-attribution` | industry reports; experts argue; experts agree; studies show; observers have cited | Structures |
| `stock-testimonial` | Sarah Johnson, John Smith; placeholder avatar services | Proof tells |
| `placeholder-text` | lorem ipsum; bracketed "Your" or "Insert"; Your Company Name; as an AI (language) model; default titles | Placeholder leftovers |
| `arrow-in-button` | an arrow glyph ending a button or link label | Call-to-action tells |
| `emoji-bullet` | an emoji opening a heading or list item | Call-to-action tells |

To run the scan, when the landing-skills plugin is installed, from the
plugin's root directory (`landing-skills:landing-review` owns the script):

```bash
node skills/landing-review/scripts/detect-tells.mjs path/to/landing/copy.md
```

It exits 0 when it finds nothing. The scan is optional; reading the copy
against this file by eye is the required check.
